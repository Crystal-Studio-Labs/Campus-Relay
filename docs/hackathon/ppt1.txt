## Slide 6: The Requester Verification Guard
**Subtitle**: Eliminating Ghost Closures & Enforcing Real Operational Accountability  
**Presenter**: Manas Kumar Mallick · Case Lifecycle Engine & Verification  

### The Ghost Closure Problem
- In traditional ERPs, staff mark complaints 'Closed' to protect their metrics while issues persist.
- Administration is misled by fake 99% SLA completion statistics.

### The Requester Verification Guard
- Technicians can ONLY move a case to 'RESOLVED'—never directly to 'CLOSED'.
- The case moves to 'VERIFICATION_REQUIRED', notifying the student immediately.
- If verified: Student taps 'Verify & Close' ➔ Case permanently closes.
- If issue persists: Student taps 'Reopen Issue' ➔ Ticket resets to 'IN_PROGRESS' with an SLA penalty.

### Automated SLA Sweep Engine
- Background SLA worker continuously monitors resolution deadlines.
- Automatically shifts case states: ACTIVE ➔ AT_RISK (75% time elapsed) ➔ BREACHED (100% elapsed).
- Breaches immediately alert the Warden and Registrar on their command dashboards.

**Visual Style**: Side-by-side comparison on frosted glass. Left: Interactive student prompt with glowing Emerald "Verify & Close" button vs. Sunburst Amber "Reopen" button. Right: Glowing circular glass SLA timer gauge showing real-time breach status.  
**Speaker Notes**: "In legacy software, technicians routinely mark complaints 'closed' just to make their departmental metrics look good, while the student's tap continues to flood the room. Campus Relay makes this impossible with our Requester Verification Guard. Staff can only mark an issue as 'RESOLVED'. The system physically prevents them from closing it. Only the student can confirm the repair on their phone or hostel kiosk. If the tap is still leaking, one tap on 'Reopen' immediately escalates the case into the department head's priority queue and logs an SLA penalty."

## Slide 7: Local-First Offline Resilience
**Subtitle**: Zero Data Loss During Severe Wi-Fi and Cellular Outages  
**Presenter**: Pruthiraj Lenka · Frontend Engineering & Local-First PWA  

### Native IndexedDB Client Storage
- Four dedicated local stores: Outbox Queue, Response Cache, Form Drafts, and System Metadata.
- Forms submit instantly to the local outbox even when airplane mode is active.

### Replay-Safe Idempotent Synchronization
- Every offline mutation receives an immutable client-generated UUID idempotency key.
- Guarantees that retrying or repeating a network sync creates exactly ONE server record.
- Server rejects duplicate replays and safely flags concurrent state conflicts.

### Background Outbox Synchronization Engine
- Background worker continuously monitors network health via lightweight heartbeat pings.
- Automatically flushes queued outbox mutations to /api/v1/sync/push when connection restores.
- Students and staff continue working uninterrupted through campus Wi-Fi blackouts.

**Visual Style**: High-tech glassmorphic diagram. Left: Translucent smartphone vault storing glowing data packets. Center: Severed Wi-Fi icon reconnecting with electric cyan pulse. Right: PostgreSQL server database receiving synced batches without duplicates.  
**Speaker Notes**: "Thank you, Manas. College campuses are notorious for weak Wi-Fi, especially inside hostel basements or during monsoon power cuts. Most web applications crash the moment internet connectivity dies. Campus Relay was engineered local-first. Using a custom native IndexedDB wrapper, our PWA stores all forms, drafts, and cases directly on the user's device. If a student files a complaint or a guard logs an exit with zero network, it saves instantly with a unique client-side UUID. The moment the device catches even a faint 2G signal, our background engine replays the outbox. Zero data loss, zero duplicates, zero downtime."
